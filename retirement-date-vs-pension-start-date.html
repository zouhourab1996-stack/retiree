Retirement Date vs Pension Start Date

Leaving work, starting a pension and receiving the first payment can be three different events. Put each date on your calendar before choosing a retirement countdown.

Map the dates separately

DateWhy it matters
Notice dateWhen your employer receives your written notice.
Last working dayWhen you stop performing scheduled work.
Employment end dateThe date HR records for employment and final pay.
Pension commencement dateThe date the plan says your benefit begins.
First payment dateWhen money actually reaches your account.

Questions to ask the plan administrator

  • Is the pension commencement date different from the application date?
  • Is there a waiting period or missing-document risk?
  • How are partial months, final pay and unused leave handled?
  • What happens to healthcare and other benefits between dates?
  • Who confirms the application and first-payment schedule?

Use a countdown carefully

Our notice-period checklist helps map the employer side, while the healthcare gap checklist covers coverage dates. A countdown cannot determine eligibility, tax treatment or the best claiming choice.

FAQ

Can I retire before my pension starts?

Some plans allow a gap between leaving work and starting benefits, but the rules vary. Confirm with the official plan administrator.

Is the first payment made on the start date?

Not always. Payment schedules and processing times differ, so ask for the expected first-payment date in writing.
