How the paycheck count works

The counter assumes a typical biweekly pay cycle — one paycheck every 14 days, about 26 per year. If you're paid monthly, divide the biweekly count by two, then nudge it up slightly (12 months ≈ 26 biweeks / 2 + 1). If you're paid weekly, double it.

Pay cyclePer yearAdjust
Biweekly (every 2 weeks)26Use the counter as-is
Semi-monthly (1st & 15th)24Subtract about 8%
Monthly12Divide by 2, add 1
Weekly52Double it

Why the paycheck number matters

Retirement planning has a rhythm, and it's the paycheck. Each one is a last chance to contribute to a 401(k) or IRA, to crush a remaining debt, or simply to watch the finish line get one step closer. People who count paychecks instead of days tend to make different, calmer final-year decisions — the number turns "I should probably save more" into "I have exactly 22 of these left."

We don't tell you what to do with them — a fee-only financial advisor does that. We just make the number precise.
