Born in 1955: When Can You Retire?

If you were born in 1955, your full retirement age — the age for full Social Security benefits — is 66 years and 2 months. That is 66 years and 2 months after your 66th birthday.

What "full retirement age" actually decides

Full retirement age (FRA) is the baseline Social Security uses to scale your monthly benefit. Claim before it and the benefit is permanently reduced; claim after it and it grows, by roughly 8% for every year you wait, up to age 70. The FRA itself moved later for later birth years — Congress set the gradual rise in the 1983 reforms — which is why 1955-born retireeswait a little longer than the classes before them.

Medicare runs on its own clock: it starts at 65 whatever you decide about Social Security, and many people bridge the gap between 65 and their FRA with marketplace or employer coverage.

Retiring before (or after) 66 years and 2 months

Plenty of people born in 1955 retire before their FRA — savings, pensions or a working spouse can make 62 work. Others keep working past it and let delayed credits build toward the age-70 maximum. Both are legitimate; the right answer depends on health, savings and how much you like your job — a personal decision this page won't pretend to make for you.

Whatever date you choose, the counters on this site count it: the main countdown, work days and paychecks.
