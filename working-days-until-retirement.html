How the count works

The work-days number counts every Monday-to-Friday between tomorrow and your retirement date, inclusive. It does not subtract public holidays or vacation days, because those vary by country, employer and how aggressively you've been banking leave. For your true remaining number:

Your situationWhat to do
Public holidaysSubtract roughly 10–12 per year (US average)
Vacation days leftSubtract your remaining balance
Part-time or compressed weeksScale the number to your schedule

Why the work-days number feels better

Calendar days include the weekends you'd have off anyway. When retirees-to-be switch to counting work days, the number drops by about 30% — and so does the dread. A year left becomes roughly 250 work days. Five years becomes about 1,250. Suddenly it's a countable thing, not a sentence.

Planning the run-in

Knowing your work-day count lets you plan backwards: handover documents, training your replacement, the projects worth finishing versus the ones worth handing over politely. Many people also track remaining paychecks — the number that shapes final savings decisions.
