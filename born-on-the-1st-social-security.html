Born on the 1st? How Social Security Counts Your Age

Social Security counts you as reaching an age on the day before your birthday. For most people that changes nothing. If you were born on the 1st of a month it moves your full retirement date back a month, and if you were born on January 1 it can move it back two.



The rule in one sentence

Federal regulation 20 CFR 404.2(c)(4) says an individual attains a given age "on the first moment of the day preceding the anniversary of his birth." Your 67th birthday is the anniversary; you attain age 67 the day before it.

Social Security's own table notes turn that into two practical rules. If you were born on January 1, use the previous year's row. If you were born on the 1st of any other month, your full retirement age is figured as if your birthday were in the previous month. A third note says you must be at least 62 for the entire month to receive benefits for it.

Everyone born in 1960 or later has a full retirement age of 67, except people born on January 1, 1960, who use the 1959 row. These are the months Social Security counts as full retirement months for 1960 births. The last column shows the "day before" in action for someone born on the 15th.

Someone born on January 1, 1960 reached full retirement age on October 31, 2026. Someone born a day later, on January 2, reaches it on January 1, 2027. One calendar day separates them, and the rule gives them an age difference of two months.

Why other calculators may disagree with you

Simplified charts use the birth year alone, and some calculators use only the year and month. They are right for most birthdays and a month off for people born on the 1st. Social Security's own table gives you an age, not a date; this page turns it into dates.

Frequently asked questions

If I was born on the 1st, is my full retirement age earlier?

The age is the same, but Social Security treats your birthday as falling in the previous month, so you reach it a month earlier than the calendar suggests. If you were born on January 1, it uses the previous year's schedule. For a January 1, 1960 birthday that means 66 and 10 months instead of 67.

Why does Social Security use the day before my birthday?

Federal regulation 20 CFR 404.2(c)(4) says an individual attains a given age on the first moment of the day preceding the anniversary of his birth. The rest of the rules follow from that sentence.

When can I claim at 62 if I was born on the 2nd?

Social Security says you must be at least 62 for the entire month to receive benefits. Born on the 2nd, you turn 62 on the 1st, so your birthday month counts. The same is true if you were born on the 1st. From the 3rd onward, your first month is the one after your birthday month.

Does this change how much I get?

Only through timing. It shifts which month counts as your full retirement month, so it changes how many months early or late you are if you claim near that date. The reduction or credit per month does not change.
