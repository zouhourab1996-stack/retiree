Your generation is the flat plateau of the table: 66 for all twelve birth years, before the staircase up to 67 begins (ssa.gov).

What FRA = 66 meant at claiming time

If you claimed at your full retirement age, you received 100% of your primary insurance amount. Claim at 62 instead and the reduction for your generation is about 25% — permanently. Those are SSA's published rules; we state them without advising either way.

Delaying to 70: the +32% window

Delayed retirement credits add 8% per year past full retirement age — from 66 to 70, that's up to +32%. Credits stop at 70; there is never a reason to wait past it.

Medicare came at 65

Medicare eligibility is 65 for everyone — it did not wait for your FRA. If you missed your initial enrollment window (the 7 months around your 65th birthday), general enrollment runs January 1 – March 31 annually (ssa.gov/medicare).

Questions people ask

What is my full retirement age if I was born in 1950?

Exactly 66. Everyone born from 1943 through 1954 has a full retirement age of 66 — the simplest generation in the Social Security table (ssa.gov).

Is it too late to delay my benefits?

Delayed retirement credits stop at 70 — if you've already passed 70, there's no further increase to wait for. If you haven't, every month up to 70 still adds about two-thirds of one percent.

I claimed at 62 — what did that cost?

Claiming at 62 with a full retirement age of 66 permanently reduces your monthly benefit by roughly 25% compared to your full amount. The exact rules are SSA's to state (ssa.gov) — we don't give financial advice.

When did my Medicare start?

At 65, like everyone — Medicare eligibility doesn't follow your full retirement age. If you missed your initial enrollment window, the general enrollment period runs January 1 to March 31 each year (ssa.gov/medicare).

What about spousal benefits?

Spousal rules have their own ages and caps — the authoritative source is ssa.gov's spousal benefits pages. We state official rules and count days; decisions belong to you.
