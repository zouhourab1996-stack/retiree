Why people count

Ask a room of people within five years of retirement what they're doing, and someone is counting. Down to the day. Retirement forums are full of it — people posting their countdown number like a score, comparing calendar days versus working days, marking off paychecks. It's not morbid; it's momentum. A shrinking number turns an abstract "someday" into a finish line you can see.

This page exists to make that number precise — and a little more fun than a spreadsheet.

The three numbers that matter

📅 Calendar days. The classic. Every single day between today and the day you walk out — including weekends, holidays and days you'll spend wondering if the pigeon in the parking lot will miss you.
💼 Work days. The honest number. Weekdays only — the days you'll actually be working. Retirees-to-be consistently say this one feels more real (and more survivable) than the calendar count.
💵 Paychecks ≈ The number your savings plan cares about. Assuming a typical every-two-weeks pay cycle, this is roughly how many more times money lands in your account before it's pension and Social Security time.
