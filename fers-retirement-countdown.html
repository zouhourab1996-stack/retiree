Why the date matters more in government

Three official rules turn one weekday into money: the month-end rule, the leave-period rule, and the year-end cluster. Pick wrong and you leave a pay period half-accrued; pick right and the annuity starts clean.

The three FERS rules

1. End of the month. Your annuity starts the first day of the following month — retire on the 31st, not the 15th (Interior Business Center, ibc.doi.gov).
2. End of the pay period. Retire on the last day of a pay period to complete your accrual — no partial hours left on the table.
3. End of the year. Roughly 40% of federal retirements happen in the year-end holiday period: the leave lump sum plus a January annuity start line up perfectly.

The leave payout

Your unused annual leave is paid as a lump sum roughly 45 days after retirement — and long-tenured employees can carry 400+ accrued hours into that payout. It's the biggest single check most feds ever see from a paycheck system.

Questions people ask

What's the best time of year for a FERS employee to retire?

For most FERS employees, the last day of a pay period in the last third of the year — ideally December. You complete the leave period, the annuity starts the first of the next month, and you bank the holiday-period payout.

Why do about 40% of federal retirements happen at year end?

The end-of-year cluster combines three payoffs: the holiday period (paid leave), the annual-leave lump sum paid out after separation, and a clean January annity start. OPM data consistently shows the spike.

What are the best FERS retirement dates in 2027?

Per Federal News Network (September 2026): January 9, April 30, May 29, October 30 and December 31 — each lands on a pay-period end plus a month-end bonus.

When does my FERS annuity start?

The first day of the month after you retire — retire January 31, annuity starts February 1. That's why retiring on the last day of a month is the baseline FERS rule (DOI/IBC).

When do I get my annual leave payout?

Roughly 45 days after retirement, as a lump sum in your final paycheck cycle — long-tenured employees can see 400+ hours of accrued leave paid out.
